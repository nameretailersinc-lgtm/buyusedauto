# How Much Does It Cost to Charge an Electric Car at Home or in Public?

Electric cars use electricity instead of gasoline, but asking **“How much does it cost to charge an EV?”** is similar to asking how much it costs to fill a fuel tank. The answer depends on the amount of energy added and the price charged by your electricity provider or charging network.

You can estimate the cost of a charge without knowing the car's brand. You need the battery capacity, how full the battery is before and after charging, and the relevant electricity price. A more realistic estimate also allows for charging losses.

## The basic EV charging-cost formula

A simple starting formula is:

**Battery energy added (kWh) = usable battery capacity (kWh) × (target charge % − starting charge %)**

Then:

**Estimated billed energy (kWh) = battery energy added ÷ charging efficiency**

**Estimated electricity cost = billed energy × price per kWh**

Charging efficiency accounts for energy that does not reach the battery. When you do not know it, use a clearly labeled adjustable assumption rather than presenting one constant as universal.

## Example: charging from 20% to 80%

Imagine an EV with a **60 kWh usable battery**, a charge that starts at **20%**, a target of **80%**, an illustrative **90% charging efficiency**, and electricity priced at **$0.18 per kWh**.

1. Battery energy added: 60 × (0.80 − 0.20) = **36 kWh**.
2. Energy purchased after assumed losses: 36 ÷ 0.90 = **40 kWh**.
3. Charging cost: 40 × $0.18 = **$7.20**.

That example is not a quoted charging price or a real-world average. A charger that applies a session fee, time-based billing, taxes, or a variable rate may cost more. Charging speed can also vary during a session.

## Cost at home versus public charging

At home, you might pay a flat rate or an electricity rate that changes by time of day. Some utilities offer lower prices for charging during certain hours, while others may have demand charges or additional conditions. Check the tariff that actually applies to your household.

Public stations may price a charge by kWh, time connected, session, or another combination, where permitted. A fast charger can be convenient on a road trip but may be more expensive than home charging. Check the network's posted terms before plugging in.

| Factor | Home charging | Public charging |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price source | Household utility tariff | Charging-network tariff |
| Fees to include | Electricity plus possible equipment costs | Energy or time fees, possible session/parking fees |
| Main advantage | Convenience when available | Access away from home |
| Main limitation | Parking and installation availability | Variable pricing, availability, and charging speed |

## How to calculate electric-car cost per mile

EV efficiency is often given in **kWh per 100 miles**. Assume your vehicle uses **30 kWh per 100 miles** and your effective electricity price is **$0.18 per kWh**, excluding additional charging losses:

**Energy cost per mile = (30 ÷ 100) × $0.18 = $0.054**, or approximately **5.4 cents per mile**.

To include losses, increase electricity purchased according to the charging-efficiency assumption. You can then compare that figure with a gasoline car's cost per mile based on its miles per gallon and the price of fuel.

Remember that energy is only part of ownership cost. Insurance, tires, depreciation, financing, and maintenance can change the overall comparison.

## What affects the accuracy of the estimate?

- The difference between **usable** and **gross** battery capacity.
- Temperature, battery conditioning, and charging equipment losses.
- The charge level you start from and the level you want to reach.
- Differences between home and public electricity rates.
- Per-minute, parking, idle, and session fees.
- Vehicle efficiency, traffic, speed, and driving conditions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is charging from 20% to 80% cheaper than a full charge?

It adds less energy, so the energy portion of the bill is typically lower, assuming the same price per kWh. The exact cost depends on losses and any added fees.

### How much does it cost to charge an EV for 100 miles?

Multiply the vehicle's kWh per 100 miles by the price per kWh, and adjust for any relevant charging losses or fees.

### Is home charging always cheaper?

Not always. It often can be, but the comparison depends on local tariffs, network prices, the time of charging, and equipment costs.

### Can I calculate a full charge using battery size alone?

You also need the starting charge level, energy price, and a realistic allowance for losses. A complete estimate should include any additional charging fees.
